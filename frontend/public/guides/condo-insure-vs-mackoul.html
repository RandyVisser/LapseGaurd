condo.insure vs. Mackoul Building Guard

Both track whether unit owners carry the insurance your declaration requires. Building Guard is a monitoring service delivered by Mackoul's staff — an established insurance agency doing the work for you. condo.insure is software you run yourself, with published per-unit pricing and AI that reads each declaration page. The right answer depends on whether you want it handled or want to see it.

Who they are

Mackoul Risk Solutions describes itself as "an independent insurance agency" specializing in real estate, business, commercial and personal insurance, "insurance monitoring," and employee benefits.[1] It was founded in 1987, lists 64 employees, and states it is "proudly insuring over 1,900 community associations."[2] Offices are in Long Beach NY, Morristown NJ and Chicago IL, serving "clients throughout the country."

Building Guard is their insurance-monitoring program, run on its own site and phone line.[3] It covers "the owners, renters, and commercial occupants in your building or association" using, in their words, "our specialized software and dedicated department."[3]

condo.insure is software for condominium boards and property management firms, built Florida-first around HO-6 compliance. Owners submit by forwarding the email their insurer already sent; AI extracts the insurer, policy number, expiration date and coverage limits and checks them against the association's requirements.

On price — what can and can't be said

Straight answer

We cannot tell you whether we are cheaper, because Mackoul does not publish a price. Their site invites you to "get a free quote for your association" and describes a one-year contract.[4] Anyone claiming to know their rate without a quote is guessing.

What we can say is what we charge, publicly, before you talk to anyone: $1.00 per unit per month up to 750 units, $0.50 from 751 to 10,000, $0.25 above that, with a $50 per month minimum, a 90-day free trial and no setup fee. A 120-unit association pays $120 a month. You can work out your own number on the cost calculator without giving us an email address.

That difference — a published rate versus a quote — is the honest pricing comparison. It is not a claim about which is less expensive.

Where Mackoul is the better choice

Genuinely, and not as a rhetorical device. Pick them if:

  • You want it handled, not shown to you. Building Guard is staffed work. If your board has no appetite to run anything, "a dedicated department does it" is a real answer and software isn't.
  • Mackoul already writes your master policy. One relationship, one point of contact, and the people chasing owner coverage already understand the building's insurance.
  • You need renters and commercial occupants monitored too. They state all three groups explicitly.
  • You're outside Florida. They serve clients nationally. We are built Florida-first — wind-excluded HO-6 pairs, loss assessment, post-Surfside requirements — and that focus is less useful in Ohio.
  • You want a human on the phone. A 39-year-old agency with 64 people and 1,900 associations is a different kind of counterparty than a small software company.

Where condo.insure is the better choice

  • You want to see the answer right now. A live dashboard beats a report cadence when someone asks "are we covered?" in a meeting.
  • You want to know the price before you talk to anyone. Published rate, free trial, cancel any time.
  • You manage multiple associations. One login across the portfolio, with role-based access and assignment groups, instead of a separate engagement per building.
  • You don't want owners creating accounts. Forwarding the insurer's email is the entire workflow — no portal, no password.
  • Your building has Florida's awkward policy structures. A wind-excluded HO-6 plus a separate wind-only policy is a compliant pair, and we treat it as one.
They are not mutually exclusive

Compliance tracking is one line item in an association's obligations. Plenty of boards keep their broker relationship and run software for visibility. If Mackoul writes your master policy, that is not a reason to avoid tracking coverage yourself — and vice versa.

How to check any of this yourself

  1. Ask both for a written scope: who uploads owners, who chases them, how often, and what the board receives.
  2. Ask what happens to a policy that is current but non-compliant — wrong limits, wind excluded. That is where most tracking quietly fails.
  3. Ask for the contract term and what renewal looks like.
  4. Ask whether owners must create an account. Response rates depend on it more than anything else.
  5. Get Mackoul's quote. We can't tell you the number and neither can anyone else.
